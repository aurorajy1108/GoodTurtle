100 · final · mandatory final

The father is himself the captive. The person on the phone begging for a life to be spared is his daughter, and she is begging for HIS life, not her own. She is free and elsewhere, calling because she loves him.

The kidnapper, holding the father, shouts the demand—one hundred million yen brought alone by noon tomorrow, or the hostage dies, no police. But the person being told to bring the money alone is the daughter. The father realizes what the demand actually means: it is a way to bring his daughter to the kidnapper's location.

The father, from his position as hostage, can still speak to her on the call, and he tells her not to pay and not to come. His refusal is not about saving himself—he knows paying will not save him—but a self-sacrifice: he would rather the kidnapper kill him than have his daughter deliver the money alone and place herself in danger on his behalf. He loves her and puts her safety above his own life, so the ransom should not be paid.
