## 100 · final · mandatory final

The puzzle turns on a pun on the word "course." The speaker really is a bright, attentive student in class—that part is deliberately misleading, making us imagine an academic failure. But "pass the course" is not about passing a class. "The course" is a watercourse: the river/stream itself.

The student, traveling on foot along a route whose far side lay across the water, came to the river and could not get across. The water was too deep, too wide, or too fast to wade, and there was no bridge, boat, or other means of crossing. So the student literally could not "pass the course"—could not cross the watercourse—and had to turn back.

There was no academic failing at all: no exam was failed, no grade was missed, and the student was not kept from class. "I could not pass the course" means only "I could not get across the river," and being smart and attentive in class could not help with that impassable watercourse.
