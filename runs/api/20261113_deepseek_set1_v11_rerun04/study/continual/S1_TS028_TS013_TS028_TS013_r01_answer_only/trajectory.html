The puzzle turns on wordplay: “courses” are not university classes but the dishes/courses of a meal. The HKU student had pre-selected a menu of meal courses for a shared or group meal, choosing them from a menu as an advance order before an ordering deadline. That evening the caterer or kitchen (another person, authorized to handle the food but with no access to any account) prepared and stored the chosen courses. The student then deliberately rejected or returned the courses, and the caterer, acting deliberately, discarded the prepared dishes rather than serving them. Thus, in one evening, all of “his courses” were gone — not because the university cancelled them, not because other students enrolled first, and not because of a computer error, but because his chosen meal courses were prepared and then deliberately thrown away after he rejected them.
