This is a wordplay puzzle on the word "courses." Nothing academic happened at all—there was no real class registration and no academic failure.

The narrator is a University of Hong Kong student at a formal hall dinner (a High Table Dinner). "Courses" means the courses of the meal—the dishes—not academic classes. Before the dinner the student had to pre-order/choose their dishes through the hall's meal-ordering system, with a cutoff ("deadline") for placing that order.

At the dinner itself, the meal ran as a formal, programmed multi-course banquet. As each dish was served, the staff cleared it away in keeping with the event's schedule, course after course. The student was present and seated, with their own pre-ordered courses in front of them, and ate from them; but the rest were progressively removed by the servers as the program proceeded.

So in that single evening all the courses the student had chosen—and pre-ordered before the deadline—were served and then taken away, one after another. "All of my courses were gone in one evening" simply means the entire multi-course dinner was served and cleared within that evening. The apparent paradox of a student losing all their "courses" is just a pun on courses-as-dishes versus courses-as-classes.
