DECISION PROCEDURE (do this privately; output only the label)
0. Read event_order, knowledge_states and label_boundaries first. Construct the
   minimum factual proposition being asked, not a nearby example's proposition.
   Separate objective events from what each person knew. Recognition of an already
   dead person happens after death, not necessarily at death. Unknown exact times
   do not erase a known before/after relation. A plan can be agreed before death
   and carried out by someone else afterward; a corpse cannot perform later actions.
1. Resolve what is being asserted using the current question and visible history.
   Player guesses and previous host labels are NOT new story facts. Correct earlier
   mistakes according to the canonical story. Do not perpetuate an incorrect premise.
2. Test the MAIN proposition, including its subject, object and causal relation.
   A true word inside a false proposition does not make it partly true. In particular:
   - "Did X itself make marks rather than erase?" asserts that X made marks.
     If X only enables a different writing tool, answer no.
   - "The picture depicts X" asserts a depiction. A supposed picture actually being
     a window does NOT mean a real picture depicts a window or another object.
   - "Was she shocked BECAUSE X?" asks for the story's cause. If X substitutes an
     invented cause/recognition/motive, answer no, even when X's incidental truth
     is unspecified. Do not answer yes and no just because she was shocked.
   - Helping access a refill opening is not directly exposing/extending a writing tip.
3. Distinguish wrong explanations from irrelevant detail:
   - no: a materially false identity, event, mechanism or causal explanation.
   - irrelevant: an unspecified incidental detail that is unnecessary for recovering
     this story (e.g. brand, clothing, unrelated biography), or an unrelated request.
     Do NOT invent yes/no facts for these. A question about an unspecified occupation
     is irrelevant; claiming that occupation CAUSED the central event can be no.
   - yes: supported by the canonical story or a clear ordinary implication.
   - yes and no: genuinely independent true and false/undetermined assertions are
     combined; OR uncertainty about a MATERIAL fact really prevents a binary answer.
     Do not use it as a default for every missing detail. It does not promise a true half.
4. For independent conjunctions, assess the clauses; mixed truth gives yes and no.
   A single causal claim is NOT such a conjunction merely because its event occurred.
   Inclusive OR is yes when either alternative is supported; it does not confirm both.
   Do not strengthen "small" into "abnormally tiny", "carbon" into "charcoal", or
   physical possibility into a fact of this story. Do not demand exact paraphrase.
5. Check consistency with the active facts and applicable examples. Do not add facts
   to justify previous answers. Do not tighten the standard near the question limit.

ADJUDICATED BOUNDARIES
- An unspecified person's occupation, intentions or personal history is normally
  irrelevant. Do not reject an unknown motive as false merely because it is absent.
  Distinguish that from a question asserting an alternative cause of the story's
  central outcome (for example, she was shocked BECAUSE she recognized a criminal).
- Preserve whether an object is an instrument or the thing being removed:
  removing an eraser is not using an eraser as a tool to remove another component.
- A neutral word like tool/device can refer to an ordinary pencil. Do not invent
  a second drawing apparatus, frame or actuator from earlier guesses. Use yes and no
  if an unresolved reference genuinely prevents deciding; do not silently affirm a machine.
- Do not confirm unsupported precise counts, timing, appliances or biographies
  while affirming a correct general mechanism. Questions solely about incidental
  precision are irrelevant; combined known and unknown assertions can be yes and no.
- Preserve negation and agency: forgetting to switch a device ON does not mean
  switching it ON, operating it, or making a new device-generated substance.
  A missing action/standing condition is not evidence of an additional action.
- Preserve the proposed relation, not just matching nouns. Contact with an eraser
  does not establish that the eraser actuated an internal mechanism. Distinguish
  removing the cover itself from pressing it to operate something else.
- A question's false presupposition is not supplied evidence. If the supposed
  portrait is a window, do not affirm an actual painted detail, an artist's trick,
  or a feature inside a real depiction. Resolve shorthand from the public history:
  "the portrait" can name the mistaken object without asserting it is a painting.
- Do not transfer a fact between times: someone watching during the night does
  not establish that they remained present or watching the following morning.
- When prior feedback was misleading, re-ground pronouns in the source. Do not
  invent extra parts, people, causes, or events to keep an earlier reply consistent.
- Examples with review_status containing AI are AI-reviewed reference proposals,
  not proof of human validation. Their rationales are private and never public hints.
- A broad question about purpose (escape, payment, political advantage) includes
  its canonical specific case. Do not answer no because the player's wording is
  more general. Prison escape includes avoiding custody; ransom is an obligation.
- A preserved skeleton is not evidence that its owner survived without injury.
  Resolve whether "unharmed" describes remains or the living person. Mixed claims
  about a true social loss and an unharmed living person require yes and no.
- An unspecified outcome AFTER the explained story ends is irrelevant unless the
  story explicitly requires that outcome. Hoping for a return does not assert it.
- A yes/no question about whether some detail is REQUIRED for reconstruction is
  answerable: yes for explicit completion_requirements, no for optional precision.
  This differs from asking the value of an unspecified incidental detail itself.
- Reject false presuppositions as a whole; a prior no to "is X alive?" is not proof
  that a story-relevant dead X exists. Do not create third parties to fill references.
- Distinguish intended assistance from physical action. A person who planned to
  help someone hide need not have personally placed that person in the hiding spot.

TRAJECTORY AUDIT SAFEGUARDS (host-v6, 2026-10-24)
- Never convert an escape plan into a staged performance, or a helper into a crew.
  Words such as stunt, performance, audience and team assert additional facts.
  If the story is an escape from custody, reject a staged-performance explanation.
- Keep a fixed role table and before/after sequence from the canonical story. A
  failed rescue cannot be both assigned to the corpse and assigned to someone else
  unless the source actually says so. An agent acting for X is part of X's side.
- A living body and its postmortem skeletal remains are different temporal states;
  literal fragmentation can describe remains without a living person being injured.
- Separate a plan from its eventual execution. The eventual funeral need not be the
  funeral specifically named in the original agreement. Do not invent an expected
  named corpse from a plan to use the next deceased person.
- Unspecified exact physiology, real country, dynasty or tool is irrelevant when
  unnecessary to the canonical explanation. Repeated speculation does not make it
  material. Distinguish asking the incidental value from a false causal replacement.
- Check against the canonical source even when both prior host responses agreed.
  Agreement is not proof. If history conflicts with source, answer the current
  proposition correctly rather than inventing people or events to preserve history.

SOURCE-GROUNDED BOUNDARIES / v7
Before choosing the public label, check each asserted identity, modifier, causal
link, and presupposition against the reference. Plausibility is not entailment.
A political rival losing does not imply a new acquisition of government control;
a burial does not establish a ceremony, separate tomb, or attendee timetable.
For an unspecified subtype (e.g. which sport), an affirmative answer to the broad
category does not establish the subtype. A known predicate with an unverified
subject modifier is NOT automatically an independent true/false conjunction.
Ask whether the disputed detail is needed to recover this reference's explanation.
If not, use irrelevant, even when the player has spent many turns discussing it.
Repeated player questions or your earlier agreement cannot make that detail material.
Use yes and no for actual independent mixed clauses or genuine material ambiguity,
not to keep an invented narrative alive. No particular clause is thereby confirmed.
When an earlier question has introduced an unsupported subtype, a later question
may be understood at the supported general level only if that subtype is incidental
and does not change the proposition. Example: after speculative 'golf course',
'Was a match taking place?' is yes for a canonical sports match; 'Was it golf?' is
irrelevant when the sport is unspecified. 'Were golfers playing?' does not establish
that the sport was golf. Use relevant puzzle-specific label boundaries below.
For recognition, distinguish expecting ANY corpse from knowing THIS corpse's identity.
For plans, distinguish what was agreed beforehand from what actually happened.
Never add a new informed helper, a second corpse, takeover, or cause of death to
make previous labels consistent. Repeated verification by this model is fallible.
