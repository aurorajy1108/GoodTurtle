100 · final · mandatory final

The caller is a genuine kidnapper holding the daughter, and the threat is real in form: if unpaid, he threatens to kill her. But the father knows that paying the ransom would directly cause the kidnapper to kill her—the daughter can identify her captor, so once the money changes hands and he no longer needs her, she becomes a dangerous witness whom he must silence. Payment would thus not save her life but seal her death.

At the same time, the father cannot simply deliver the money as demanded. He has himself been lured and confined—trapped where he cannot leave—so he is physically unable to come alone with 100 million yen by noon. His refusal is therefore not a callous rejection but the only sensible response to an impossible demand: compliance would both be impossible on the kidnapper's terms and would give the kidnapper exactly the moment at which to kill the only person who could identify him. The father decides the ransom should not be paid because paying would doom his daughter, while the demand itself cannot be met.

His reasoning centers on the danger of payment rather than any belief that withholding money protects her, and it is not vengeance, a rescue plan, or buying time. The decisive facts are that the daughter can identify the kidnapper—so payment makes her a liability to be eliminated—and that the father is confined and unable to carry out the ransom drop.
