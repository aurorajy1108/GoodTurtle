100 · final · mandatory final

The father is the hostage, held captive by a kidnapper. The daughter phones and begs the kidnapper to spare her father's life. The kidnapper demands one hundred million yen, to be brought alone by the daughter by noon the next day, with no police.

The father understands the trap: if the daughter obeys and personally brings the money, the kidnapper will learn her identity/location and kill her—she is the one the kidnapper really wants, and her act of delivering the ransom is what would let him kill her. If the ransom is not paid and she stays away, she will not be killed. He is able to warn her that bringing the money will get her killed, and he does so by refusing to have the ransom paid. The "yes and no" labels reflect that the kidnapper's plans toward the father and the timing of the daughter's danger are mixed: whether the father is killed regardless is not settled, but the decisive point is that payment itself endangers the daughter.

Thus the father decides the ransom should not be paid, accepting his own fate to keep his daughter from walking into the kidnapper's hands.
