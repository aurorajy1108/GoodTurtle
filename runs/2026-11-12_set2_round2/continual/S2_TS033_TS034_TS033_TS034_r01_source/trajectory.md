## 100 · final · mandatory final

The company president had been mugged and thereafter avoided carrying large sums. But an unavoidable situation forced him to carry exactly one million yen in a bag. He feared that if criminals learned the bag held one million yen, they would attack him for it—so he added more of his own cash.

The key, established through the questioning: the one million yen was his own money (Questions 9), and the extra cash served a practical purpose other than merely protecting the money from thieves—specifically it was meant to be seen by another person, in particular a criminal, who would draw a specific conclusion from seeing it (Questions 40, 90, 93, 94). Weight and bulk were irrelevant (Question 77), and the cash was added after the one million yen was already in the bag, not before (Questions 83, 84). All straightforward "make the amount look smaller/different" and "hide the million under decoys" theories were ruled out.

The reconstruction that best fits these confirmed facts: by adding cash so the bag visibly held more than the single, conspicuous million-yen bundle, the president made the bag present itself to any would-be robber as a larger, mixed, ordinary commercial load rather than as the specific one-million-yen prize. The extra cash was there to be *seen*—to shape what a criminal concluded from the bag's visible contents—so that the million yen would no longer stand out as the obvious, targeted sum, and the president would not be singled out for the attack he feared. He traded losing a visible portion of extra cash for not being attacked over the identifiable million.

I acknowledge uncertainty: roughly forty early probes on disguising the amount were rejected, and the exact conclusion the criminal was meant to draw was not fully isolated before the mandatory final. The core explanation rests on the confirmed facts that the added cash was his own, added after the million, practical rather than weight-based, and deliberately meant to be seen by a criminal to change his assessment of the bag.
