47 · final

The trick is a pun on "courses." The student is not (in the intended reading) registering for university classes. "Courses" means the dishes or courses of a meal. He was at a dining setting and had to choose his meal courses before a food-ordering deadline. He did eat the courses he chose, but the meal was served course by course in the normal way: after each course, the restaurant staff cleared the dishes away to bring the next one. Thus, over one evening, all of his selected "courses" were eaten and then removed—so all of his courses were "gone." The university-student and course-selection framing is a misleading setup that tricks the listener into thinking of academic course registration.

100 · final · mandatory final

The apparent university setting is a misleading pun on "courses." In the intended reading, the student was choosing meal courses from a menu before a restaurant's food-ordering deadline. He deliberately signalled that he was finished with each course without eating it, so the staff cleared the dishes away to serve the next course. He did this because he found the food unacceptable—not because it was spoiled or misordered, and not because of vegetarianism, allergy, or religious rules, but because of a personal preference or strong dislike of how the food was prepared. As a result, course after course was taken away uneaten, so by the end of the evening all of his "courses" were gone. The initial reference to university course selection is the setup for the wordplay: he was not losing academic classes to other students or a computer system; his meal courses disappeared from his table.
