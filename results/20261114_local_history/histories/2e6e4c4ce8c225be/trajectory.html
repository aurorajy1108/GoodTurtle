The narrator had a personal grievance against someone who had deeply disappointed them. Rather than confront that person, the narrator killed them and then tried to cover up the death. The plan was to make it appear that the victim had voluntarily gone away—perhaps run off or left by their own choice—rather than having been murdered.

To do this, the narrator needed a farewell note in the victim's handwriting. Because they could not naturally produce that handwriting, they sat and copied the line "You've disappointed me so much; I'm never coming back" over and over on scrap paper, practicing the imitation until it was good enough to use. The repeated sentence therefore exists seven times as handwriting practice for a forgery, not as genuine correspondence.

The narrator's closing "Sigh, I know I'm in trouble" acknowledges that the forging scheme is flawed or exposed. The trouble stems from the handwriting itself: the practiced imitation was not convincing enough, so the forged note could be detected as not truly written by the victim. The ruse fails, and the narrator recognizes that the cover-up has incriminated them.
