The father is himself the hostage. A real kidnapper is holding him and has forced this smartphone call; on the line his daughter begs for his life while the kidnapper demands one hundred million yen, brought by the daughter alone by noon tomorrow, with no police.

The father refuses to have the ransom paid because his chief concern is his daughter’s safety, not his own release. By the terms of the demand, she would have to act as the courier and come to the exchange—the dangerous place where he is being held—exposing her directly to the criminal. He believes that at such a handoff she could be harmed or killed, including to silence a witness, and the evidence supports that he sees the exchange itself as the danger rather than merely the original abduction. He would rather sacrifice his own life than let her walk into that danger.

His refusal, made while the kidnapper can hear, is therefore a way to prevent her from coming with the money at all: paying would not reliably save him, but it would place his daughter in grave peril.
