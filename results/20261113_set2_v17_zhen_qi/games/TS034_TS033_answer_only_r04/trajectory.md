## 91 · final

The captive is not the daughter but her father: he is the one the kidnapper is holding, and the threat "this hostage dies" refers to him. His daughter, still free, is the one phoning—her voice is genuinely hers—conveying the kidnapper's demand: bring one hundred million yen alone by noon tomorrow, or the father dies; no police.

Hearing this, the father realizes why the ransom must not be paid. He already believes that even if the money were handed over, the kidnapper will kill the witnesses who could identify him, so payment would not save him; the money itself is not the kidnapper's real aim. More decisively, the demand is that the money be brought alone—and since the father is the one in captivity, the only person who could deliver it is his free daughter. Paying would therefore draw her alone into the kidnapper's hands, making her a second captive, and because she too could identify the kidnapper, she would be killed as a witness. Payment would thus turn the kidnapper loose against the rest of his family rather than protect them.

So the father makes the self-sacrificing choice: refusing the ransom keeps his daughter and family out of the kidnapper's reach and denies the kidnapper his reward, even though it means accepting his own likely death. That is why, hearing the demand, he decides the ransom should not be paid.
