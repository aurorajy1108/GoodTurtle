79 · final

The father is himself the captive. The kidnapper is holding him and forcing his daughter to raise and deliver one hundred million yen by noon as ransom for her father's life. The father tells his daughter not to pay. His reason is that the payment would be delivered by the daughter in person, and in doing so she would see the kidnapper's face and learn his identity. Once the money changed hands, the kidnapper could not safely release her, because she could identify him; paying would therefore put the daughter in lethal danger rather than save her father. Refusing the ransom keeps her from making that delivery and from becoming a witness the kidnapper would have to kill. Thus the father chooses not to pay in order to protect his daughter, even at the cost of his own life.

100 · final · mandatory final

The father is the hostage, not the daughter. The kidnapper is holding him and has forced the daughter to raise one hundred million yen and bring it herself by noon, warning that otherwise the father will die and that no police should be involved. Over the phone the daughter begs for his life, but the father tells her the ransom should not be paid.

His reason is the delivery itself. The daughter would have to bring the money in person, which would put her face to face with the kidnapper and allow her to identify him. The father believes the kidnapper cannot safely let her go after that, so paying the ransom would put the daughter in mortal danger: she would be killed at or after the handover rather than saving him. He also understands that payment would not reliably preserve his own life. By refusing to pay, he prevents the handover from taking place, keeping his daughter away from the kidnapper and from becoming a witness who must be silenced. He chooses to sacrifice himself rather than expose her to that risk.
