96 · final

The phrase is a wordplay. The “student” is a very smart, attentive pupil, but “course” does not mean a class here—it means a watercourse, i.e., a river. To “pass the course” therefore means to cross the river. The student could not cross it because the current was too strong and dangerous, so they failed to “pass the course.” Their intelligence and attentiveness in class are irrelevant; the failure is simply that the river could not be crossed.
